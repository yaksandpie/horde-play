How the Horde works

The Horde has infinite mana, no hand, and no decisions. Each turn it casts cards straight off the top of its library in waves. Tokens are shuffled in with everything else, so they ride along inside a wave — the wave ends on a wave-ender, which is an uncommon or better, or a non-token card, depending on the ruleset you pick when the game starts.

Its creatures have haste and are ; only creatures with can block. Damage to the Horde mills that many cards and lifegain does nothing. Survivors share one life total — 100, minus 15 for each player past the first.

You win when its library and its board are both empty. Triggered and activated abilities are still yours to read and apply — the card is shown large for exactly that reason.

Rules follow hordemagic.com, including the recommended rule that a legendary milled by damage enters the battlefield and phases out.

Multi-library decks: the D&D dungeon is three levels played in order, and Angels & Demons runs two libraries at once. Each is listed here as its own deck — run them in separate games (or two screens) and treat the pair as beaten only when both are empty.

Banned combos

    Why a card gets banned

    Anything that renders the Horde ineffectual is out. A permanent that swings the board for a turn, or on the way in, is fine; a static ability that keeps the Horde's board permanently clear isn't. The published rules of thumb:

        Share this game

        Viewers get the board, the wave and the counters, with no controls to press. Give the code out the way you'd give out a password.

        Sharing sends the board to ntfy.sh, a free public relay, so viewers on other devices can read it. The code is the only thing protecting the game, and it works both ways — anyone holding it can send to the game as well as watch it, so treat it as something you hand to your table and not to a group chat. Nothing is sent until you start, and the app can’t share while it’s offline.
